# Locked Project Writing Standard

## 1. Control Information

- **Project:** Design and Implementation of a Smart Clinic Appointment and Patient Queue Management System
- **Standard:** Hybrid University + APA + Software/ML
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Effective date:** 24 September 2026
- **Status:** Locked for Chapter Three onward by owner instruction

Once approved, this file governs every chapter and may change only when the
project owner explicitly authorises a revision or supplies a conflicting
mandatory institutional rule. Record the version, date, reason, and affected
sections for every approved change.

## 2. Evidence and Claim Rules

- Use the current repository, approved objectives, verified data, test reports,
  model artefacts, screenshots, and institutional rules as sources of truth.
- Never fabricate features, participants, surveys, interviews, field tests,
  datasets, metrics, citations, screenshots, deployments, approvals, or claims.
- Distinguish completed implementation, literature findings, planned work, and
  untested assumptions.
- Preserve one-to-one alignment between objectives, research questions,
  methodology, results, and conclusions.
- State safety, professional, ethical, and validation boundaries explicitly.
- Use present tense for established knowledge/current system behaviour and past
  tense for procedures performed and results obtained.
- Write formal, natural British English without hype or filler.

## 3. Default Formatting

The corrected Chapter One is the available supervisor-approved presentation
authority and overrides conflicting defaults. Use:

- A4 portrait paper;
- left margin 1.25 inches; top/right/bottom 1 inch;
- Times New Roman 12 pt black body text;
- justified paragraphs, 1.5 line spacing, 0.5-inch first-line indent;
- 0 pt before and 0 pt after body paragraphs, following the approved chapters;
- chapter label and title: 12 pt bold uppercase centred;
- numbered first-, second-, and third-level headings: 12 pt bold left;
- 10–11 pt table/figure text;
- restrained decimal section numbering;
- lower-case Roman preliminary pages, title counted but number hidden;
- Chapter One restarting Arabic page 1 and continuing through references and
  appendices; and
- real centred Word `PAGE` fields, not typed page numbers.

Do not colour academic headings, over-number minor material, create blank space
with repeated Enter presses, or leave headings stranded at a page bottom.

## 4. Restrained Numbering

- Use numbered headings only for substantive report hierarchy.
- Present the aim as prose under an unnumbered internal label.
- Present objectives as an ordinary Arabic-numbered list.
- Number research questions to match objectives; do not make each one a
  subsection.
- Present limitations as bullets unless cross-reference requires numbering.
- Format definitions alphabetically as bold-term run-in glossary paragraphs
  with hanging indents; do not number individual terms.

## 5. Citation and Reference Standard

- Use APA 7th edition, favouring narrative citations.
- Permit sources from 2021 to the present unless an authorised
  exception is documented.
- A historical dataset or event must still be disclosed as historical; a recent
  citation cannot make old evidence appear newly collected.
- Prefer original peer-reviewed research, systematic reviews, official
  statistics, standards, legislation, and official technical documentation.
- Open and verify the actual source, DOI/URL, authors, year, title, venue,
  design, sample, scope, and supported claim.
- Keep citations close to supported claims and minimise direct quotations.
- Maintain one master reference file, not a bibliography in every chapter.
- On final compilation, remove duplicates and uncited items, confirm reciprocal
  citation coverage, and alphabetise unique entries.

## 6. Five-Chapter Structure

### Chapter One — Introduction

Background; problem; aim and measurable objectives; matching research
questions; significance; scope/exclusions; limitations; operational terms; and
summary where required.

### Chapter Two — Literature Review

Review scope; conceptual review; relevant framework; critical empirical review;
related systems/approaches; synthesis; research gap; and summary. Compare study
designs, datasets/populations, targets, validation, results, strengths,
limitations, and comparability—not accuracy percentages alone.

### Chapter Three — Methodology and System Design

Existing- and proposed-system analysis; functional and non-functional
requirements; research/development method; objective-to-method traceability;
architecture/modules; database, interface, and security design; operational
data provenance; algorithm procedure; integration; testing plan; privacy,
ethics, accessibility, and applicable disclaimers. Diagrams must match the
implemented system.

### Chapter Four — Implementation, Results and Discussion

Authentic implementation; sanitised screenshots; data/model results; software
tests; expected versus actual outcomes; objective-by-objective findings;
literature comparison; errors and limitations. Do not report unperformed field,
user, clinical, deployment, or participant studies.

### Chapter Five — Summary, Conclusion and Recommendations

Summary of problem, method, implementation, and results; achievement of each
objective; evidence-limited conclusion; recommendations; limitations; and
future work. Introduce no new result or unsupported argument.

## 7. Software and ML Reporting

Document only implemented roles, modules, endpoints, persistence, validation,
security controls, interface states, and tests. For ML/data work, report dataset
provenance and age, schema, quality, preprocessing, leakage prevention, split/
validation design, algorithm, parameters, software versions, metrics, counts,
artefact verification, interpretability, and validation boundary.

Other algorithms may be compared through verified literature but must not be
described as project experiments unless they were genuinely implemented and
their evidence exists.

## 8. Tables, Figures, Code, and Equations

- Number tables and figures by chapter.
- Introduce every table/figure in preceding prose.
- Put concise table titles above and figure captions below unless the university
  specifies otherwise.
- Use legible labels, consistent precision, explanatory/source notes, and
  sanitised screenshots.
- Keep visuals adjacent to captions and use automatic caption styles where
  possible.
- Explain symbols and metrics; move long code/output to appendices or source
  submission.

## 9. File and Approval Workflow

- Maintain one current file per chapter.
- Keep research notes separate from approved chapter files.
- Maintain a source register and master references during every chapter.
- Research, draft, audit, render, and seek approval one chapter at a time.
- Do not begin the next chapter without explicit approval unless instructed.
- Preserve approved chapters and create a new file for final compilation.

## 10. Chapter Quality Gate

Before approval confirm: required sections, objective alignment, authentic
claims, verified citations, reciprocal references, permitted years, correct
metrics/sample sizes, readable visuals, appropriate safety boundary, British/
American English consistency, formatting, pagination, accessibility, rendered
layout where tools permit, and exactly one current chapter file.
