# Research, Evidence, and Citation Standard

## Research Question to Search Plan

Before writing, translate every objective and chapter section into:

- core concept;
- synonyms and abbreviations;
- population/context;
- system/model/technology;
- outcome or evaluation concept;
- date range; and
- inclusion/exclusion criteria.

Record this plan rather than searching opportunistically.

## Source Priority

Use the strongest source appropriate to the claim:

1. current legislation, regulation, standards, and official statistics;
2. systematic reviews/meta-analyses;
3. high-quality recent primary studies;
4. authoritative books or technical guidance;
5. official documentation for the actual technology version used.

Avoid search snippets, AI summaries, anonymous tutorials, marketing claims,
Wikipedia, unverified repositories, predatory venues, and secondary citations
when the original is available.

## Verification Record

For every approved source capture:

- authors/organisation and year;
- complete title and publication;
- DOI or stable official URL;
- evidence type and study design;
- population/dataset, sample size, target/outcome, and context;
- method, validation, and metrics;
- exact claim supported;
- limitations, bias, conflicts, and comparability;
- chapter/section using it; and
- verification date.

## Critical Literature Review

Synthesis must answer what is known, how it was established, where findings
agree or conflict, why results differ, what evidence is transferable, what is
weak or missing, and how the gap justifies the present project. Do not write a
paragraph-per-paper catalogue or rank incompatible studies by accuracy.

## Authentic Project Evidence Hierarchy

When describing the project, prefer:

1. executable tests and reproduced commands;
2. machine-readable reports, migrations, artefact metadata, and hashes;
3. current source code and configuration contract;
4. controlled interface inspection using synthetic data;
5. current technical documentation; and
6. early prompts or plans.

Investigate conflicts rather than choosing the more favourable value.

## Citation Controls

- Default to narrative in-text citations when the writing standard requires it.
- Cite close to the supported statement.
- Separate literature claims from project evidence.
- Do not cite source A for a result reported only by source B.
- Do not imply causation, generalisability, external validation, or real-world
  effectiveness beyond the study design.
- Use exact locators for direct quotations and minimise quotation.
- Never invent authors, years, page numbers, DOIs, URLs, or bibliographic data.

## Final Reciprocal Audit

Create sets of all in-text author-year citations and all reference entries.
Resolve name variants, organisational abbreviations, and same-author same-year
suffixes. Require:

- every citation has exactly one matching reference;
- every retained reference is cited;
- no duplicate DOI or normalised author-year-title identity;
- all dates satisfy the approved window;
- references are alphabetised; and
- links/DOIs resolve or have a documented verification source.
